# Startup Build Plan — Idea → Product-Market Fit → Scale

A practical, step-by-step playbook. Work top to bottom. Don't skip Phase 1–3 to "just build" — that's the #1 way startups die.

**Golden rule:** Talk to customers first, spend money and build last. Fail cheap, fail fast.

---

## 0. Founding Team & Roles

You don't need many people. 2–3 cofounders is ideal. The classic trio:

| Role | Owns | Nickname |
|------|------|----------|
| **Builder** (CTO / technical) | Builds the product, owns the tech stack | "Hacker" |
| **Seller** (CEO / business) | Vision, customers, sales, fundraising | "Hustler" |
| **Designer** (Product / UX) | What to build, user experience, research | "Hipster" |

**Hire later (only after product-market fit — see Phase 4):**
- Growth / Marketing
- Customer Success / Support
- Ops / Finance
- More engineers

**Do this on day one (non-negotiable):**
- [ ] Agree equity split between cofounders — write it down
- [ ] Vesting schedule: 4 years, 1-year cliff (protects everyone if someone leaves)
- [ ] Define who is CEO (one person, final call)
- [ ] Roles & decision boundaries in writing

> Founder disputes kill more startups than competitors do. Settle this early, in writing.

## Phase 1 — Validate the Problem (before any code)

Goal: prove a real, painful problem exists for a specific group of people.

### Steps
1. **Write the problem down**, not the solution. One paragraph: who hurts, how, how often.
2. **Identify your target customer** narrowly. "Everyone" = no one. Pick a specific segment.
3. **Customer discovery interviews** — talk to 20–50 target users.
4. **Synthesize** — look for patterns. Is the pain frequent, expensive, and urgent?

### Interview rules (read *The Mom Test* by Rob Fitzpatrick)
- Ask about their **past behavior and life**, never pitch your idea.
- Bad: "Would you use an app that does X?" (everyone lies politely)
- Good: "Tell me the last time you faced [problem]. What did you do? What did it cost you?"
- Look for: money already spent, time wasted, hacky workarounds. Those = real pain.

### Interview script template
```
1. Tell me about how you currently handle [area].
2. When was the last time [problem] happened? Walk me through it.
3. What did you do about it? What did it cost you (time/money/stress)?
4. What have you tried to fix it? Why didn't that work?
5. (Don't pitch.) Who else has this problem badly?
```

### Exit criteria for Phase 1
- [ ] 20+ interviews done
- [ ] A clear, repeated pain emerges across people
- [ ] Evidence they already spend money/time trying to solve it

## Phase 2 — Validate the Market

Goal: confirm the opportunity is big enough and you can win it.

### Steps
1. **Size the market** — TAM / SAM / SOM:
   - **TAM** (Total Addressable Market) — everyone with the problem, globally.
   - **SAM** (Serviceable Available Market) — the slice you can actually serve.
   - **SOM** (Serviceable Obtainable Market) — what you can realistically capture in 1–3 years.
2. **Scan competitors and substitutes.** Include the "do nothing / spreadsheet" option — that's your real competitor.
3. **Define why you win** — your unfair advantage / wedge.

### Value proposition template
> For **[target customer]** who **[problem/need]**, our product is a **[category]** that **[key benefit]**. Unlike **[main alternative]**, we **[key differentiator]**.

### Exit criteria for Phase 2
- [ ] Market is big enough to matter (be honest)
- [ ] You know every competitor and substitute
- [ ] One sentence on why you win, that survives scrutiny

## Phase 3 — Test the Value (cheapest proof first)

Goal: get a real commitment signal **before** building the full product.

### Test ladder — cheapest to most expensive
1. **Landing page + waitlist** — describe the value, collect emails. Measures interest.
2. **Pre-sell / pre-order** — take a deposit or signed LOI. Money or signature > "I'd use that."
3. **Concierge MVP** — deliver the service manually behind the scenes (no product yet).
4. **Wizard-of-Oz** — looks automated to the user, you do it by hand.
5. **Then build the MVP** — the smallest thing that tests the core hypothesis. Weeks, not months. Ugly is fine.

### MVP scoping rule
- Write down your **single riskiest assumption.**
- Build only what's needed to test **that one thing.** Nothing else.

### Exit criteria for Phase 3
- [ ] Real commitment signal: paid deposits, signed LOIs, or strong waitlist conversion
- [ ] Riskiest assumption tested with real users

## Phase 4 — Measure, Iterate, Find Product-Market Fit (PMF)

Goal: reach the point where users **pull** the product from you.

### Metrics that matter
- **Activation** — % of new users who reach first value ("aha moment")
- **Retention** — do they come back? **This is the most important metric.**
- **Willingness to pay** — will they actually pay, and how much?
- **Referral / organic growth** — do they tell others?

> Watch the **retention curve.** If it drops to zero, no PMF. If it **flattens** at some level, you have a core of users who need this. That flattening = the strongest PMF signal.

### Iterate or pivot
- Most first ideas are partly wrong. Use data, not ego.
- **Pivot** = change one core thing (segment, problem, or solution) while keeping what's validated.
- Move fast; don't fall in love with the original plan.

### PMF signals (you'll feel it)
- [ ] Users keep coming back (retention holds)
- [ ] Organic / word-of-mouth growth appears
- [ ] Users get upset if you take it away (Sean Ellis test: >40% would be "very disappointed" without it)
- [ ] You can't keep up with demand

**Do NOT scale before PMF.** Scaling a leaky bucket burns cash and time.

## Phase 5 — Business Model & Scale (only after PMF)

Goal: build a repeatable, profitable growth engine.

### Steps
1. **Lock unit economics:**
   - **CAC** — Customer Acquisition Cost
   - **LTV** — Lifetime Value
   - Targets: **LTV > 3× CAC**, CAC payback **< 12 months**
2. **Pricing** — test tiers; price on value, not cost.
3. **Find your growth channel** — one repeatable acquisition channel that works, then double down.
4. **Decide funding path:**
   - **Bootstrap** — keep control, grow on revenue.
   - **Raise** — only to pour fuel on an engine that already works. Not to "find" PMF.
5. **Hire to fill gaps** — now is the time for the later roles in Section 0.

### Exit criteria for Phase 5
- [ ] Healthy unit economics (LTV:CAC, payback)
- [ ] At least one repeatable, scalable acquisition channel
- [ ] Clear funding plan

## Legal & Admin (run in parallel from Phase 1)

- [ ] Incorporate the company (right entity for your country/investors)
- [ ] Founder agreement + equity split + vesting (4yr / 1yr cliff)
- [ ] IP assignment — all founders/contractors assign IP to the company
- [ ] Basic accounting / bank account set up
- [ ] Terms of Service + Privacy Policy before launch
- [ ] Data/privacy compliance for your market (e.g. GDPR-style rules)

## Common Killers (avoid these)

1. **Building before validating** — solution looking for a problem.
2. **No real customer conversations** — building in a vacuum.
3. **Scaling before PMF** — pouring money into a leaky funnel.
4. **Founder conflict** — no clear equity, roles, or decision-maker.
5. **Running out of cash** — watch runway; know your burn rate.
6. **Falling in love with the idea** — ignoring data that says pivot.
7. **Vitamin, not painkiller** — "nice to have" doesn't get bought.

## One-Page Timeline (rough)

| Phase | Focus | Typical time | Key output |
|-------|-------|-------------|------------|
| 0 | Team & equity | Week 0 | Signed founder agreement |
| 1 | Problem validation | Weeks 1–4 | 20+ interviews, validated pain |
| 2 | Market validation | Weeks 3–5 | TAM/SAM/SOM, value prop |
| 3 | Value test + MVP | Weeks 4–10 | Commitment signal, MVP live |
| 4 | Iterate to PMF | Months 3–12+ | Retention + PMF signal |
| 5 | Scale | Post-PMF | Unit economics, growth engine |

> Timelines overlap and loop. Phase 4 can take many iterations. That's normal.
